1. Where & Why to Pick Baselines: Before Void vs After Elution

The purest baseline is always the pre-injection or pre-void volume buffer flow (typically 0.5 â€“ 2.5 mL for a 24 mL column), where no sample or contaminants have reached the optical cells.

Tip: Avoid post-elution baselines near the total column volume ($V_t$), which are frequently contaminated by buffer salt fronts, glycerol mismatch, or slow-leaching aggregates.

2. Linear Slope & Lamp Drift Correction

Temperature fluctuations and gradual laser warm-up can cause slight baseline drift over a 60-minute SEC run. Linear baseline subtraction connects the start and end of the peak to subtract background drift without distorting peak area.

Math: $y_{\text{corr}}(t) = y(t) - \left[y_0 + \frac{t - t_0}{t_1 - t_0}(y_1 - y_0)\right]$

3. Peak-Gated MW Artifact Suppression

SEC-MALS calculates $M_w(t) = \frac{R(0, t)}{K^* c(t)}$. When concentration $c(t) \to 0$ in empty baseline or column wash steps, dividing two near-zero noisy numbers causes mathematical divergence, creating millions of false pink MW dots!

Solution: Peak gating limits MW plotting strictly to real protein peaks where $c(t) \ge 0.05 \times c_{\text{apex}}$ and $\text{SNR} \ge 3$.

4. Dark Current & 8-Angle Photodiode Alignment

The Wyatt flow cell measures Rayleigh scattering at 8 distinct angles (32Â° to 141Â°). Each photodiode has a slight dark-current electronic offset that must be tared against pure solvent to ensure accurate angular extrapolation ($R(\theta \to 0)$).

Standard: Detector 5 (90Â°) provides the highest signal-to-noise ratio for standard globular proteins ($R_g < 10\text{ nm}$).

SEC-MALS Biophysical Educational Reference

Why MALS Needs Concentration for MW, But Can Still Measure Size (nm)

Understanding why Astra or Wyatt software halts with "Cannot calculate Molecular Weight without concentration" while still reporting particle diameters and Stokes radii in nanometers.

1. The Fundamental Rayleigh Ratio Equation for Mass

In Multi-Angle Light Scattering (MALS), the absolute excess scattered light at zero angle is given by:

R(0) = K* · c · Mw

Solving for molar mass gives Mw = R(0) / (K* · c). Because the scattered intensity is directly proportional to both concentration (c) and mass (Mw), without knowing the concentration (from UV extinction ε or differential refractive index dn/dc), the equation cannot be solved for Mw!

2. Why Particle Size (nm) Does NOT Need Concentration

Unlike molar mass, spatial dimensions are measured purely by angular phase differences and Brownian diffusion:

  • Radius of Gyration (Rg): Determined from the angular slope of light scattering across detectors: R(θ)/R(0) ≈ 1 - (16π² n²/3λ²) Rg² sin²(θ/2). The concentration c cancels out completely!
  • Hydrodynamic Radius (Rh) by DLS: Determined from the fluctuation decay rate Γ = D · q², which gives diffusion coefficient D. By the Stokes-Einstein equation Rh = kBT / (6πηD), requiring zero concentration information.
  • SEC Column Elution Volume: Molecules elute strictly according to their hydrodynamic volume (Stokes radius Rh) partitioning into pore volume (Kav). Concentration is not required to estimate size or column-apparent mass.
Biophysical Diagnostic Reference: SEC vs. MALS vs. SDS-PAGE & Aggregation

Fundamental physical differences between hydrodynamic volume elution, first-principles light scattering, and denatured electrophoresis explain why apparent retention masses diverge from true molecular weights.

1. SEC Column Calibration: Hydrodynamic Volume ($V_h$) & Stokes Radius ($R_h$)

SEC column calibration curves ($\log M_w$ vs $V_e/V_0$) assume all proteins are rigid, compact, spherical globules identical to calibration standards (e.g. BSA, Aldolase, Thyroglobulin).

Size-exclusion chromatography separates strictly by hydrodynamic volume ($V_h \propto R_h^3$), not molecular mass. Elongated, rod-like, fibrous, or intrinsically disordered proteins (IDPs) occupy a hydrodynamic envelope far larger than a sphere of equal mass. Consequently, they elute prematurely, causing column calibration to overestimate molecular weight by 200% to 300%!

Principle: Column retention volume reports effective molecular dimensions and conformational compaction, never absolute molar mass.
2. MALS: First-Principles Absolute Molar Mass ($M_w \propto R(0)/c$)

Multi-Angle Light Scattering quantifies excess Rayleigh ratio $R(0)$ extrapolated to zero angle alongside concentration $c(t)$ measured directly by refractive index (dRI) or UV absorbance:

M_w = \frac{R(0)}{K^* \cdot c} \quad \text{where} \quad K^* = \frac{4\pi^2 n_0^2 (dn/dc)^2}{N_A \lambda_0^4}

Because MALS is derived directly from first-principles Rayleigh-Debye scattering theory, it is completely independent of molecular shape, elongation, conformation, or column retention volume. No calibration standards are required.

Golden Standard: Always trust MALS over column calibration! If a peak elutes at an apparent 180 kDa on SEC but MALS reports 60 kDa, the sample is an extended/flexible 60 kDa monomer/dimer, not a 180 kDa complex.
3. SDS-PAGE vs. Native SEC-MALS: Monomer vs. Quaternary Assembly

SDS-PAGE: Heat, SDS detergent, and reducing agents (DTT/β-ME) unfold polypeptide chains and disrupt non-covalent interfaces and disulfide linkages, yielding the denatured monomer subunit mass.

SEC-MALS: Evaluates macromolecular complexes in native, non-denaturing physiological solution, providing the true intact quaternary assembly state.

Stoichiometry Example: A construct running as a single 30 kDa band on SDS-PAGE that yields $M_w = 90.5\text{ kDa}$ on SEC-MALS is definitively a native homotrimer ($3 \times 30\text{ kDa}$).
4. Aggregate Diagnostics: Soluble Void ($V_0$) vs. Trapped Insoluble Particulates

Soluble Aggregates (Void Volume $V_0$): High-MW soluble assemblies ($M_w > 1\text{â€“}5\text{ MDa}$) are larger than the matrix pore exclusion limit and elute at the column void volume ($V_0 \approx 7.5\text{â€“}8.0\text{ mL}$ for a 24 mL Superdex 200). Since Rayleigh scattering scales with $M_w$, even trace soluble aggregates ($< 0.5\%$) produce intense light scattering peaks.

Insoluble Aggregates: Sub-micron/micron particulates ($> 0.22\ \mu\text{m}$) cannot enter column channels and are retained by the 0.2 Î¼m pre-column guard filter or top column frit. They do not elute as chromatographic peaks, but rather cause gradual column backpressure elevation (MPa/psi). Centrifuge or filter (0.22 Î¼m) all samples prior to SEC-MALS.

What is dn/dc (Refractive Index Increment)?

$dn/dc$ is the rate at which a solution's refractive index changes as solute concentration increases ($mL/g$). In MALS, the optical constant $K^*$ contains $(dn/dc)^2$, while the differential refractive index concentration $c(t)$ is $\Delta n / (dn/dc)$. Therefore:

M_w = \frac{R(0)}{K^* \cdot c} \propto \frac{1}{(dn/dc)}

A 5% deviation in $dn/dc$ directly propagates into a ~5% error in absolute molar mass.

Glycoproteins: 0.165 - 0.170 mL/g

Carbohydrate chains have lower $dn/dc \approx 0.135\text{ mL/g}$, reducing the overall protein conjugate average.

Dextrans, pullulans, and neutral sugars.

Angular Fit Models & Rayleigh Scattering Extrapolation

Multi-Angle Light Scattering measures excess Rayleigh ratio $R(\theta)$ at multiple photodiodes. To eliminate destructive intra-particle interference from large molecules, the signal is extrapolated to $\theta = 0^\circ$.

1. Single Angle (90Â°) — Recommended for Globular Proteins

For particles smaller than $\sim 10\text{ nm}$ ($R_g < \lambda/20$, MW $< 1\text{ MDa}$), scattering is isotropic (identical in all directions). Single 90Â° angle provides the highest signal-to-noise ratio without extrapolation noise.

2. Zimm Model: $\frac{K^* c}{R(\theta)} = \frac{1}{M_w} \left(1 + \frac{16\pi^2 n_0^2 R_g^2}{3\lambda^2}\sin^2(\theta/2)\right)$

The classical linear extrapolation. Best for medium-sized compact complexes ($R_g \sim 15 - 50\text{ nm}$).

3. Debye Model: $\frac{R(\theta)}{K^* c} = M_w P(\theta)$

Ideal for random-coil polymers, intrinsically disordered proteins, and flexible polymers.

4. Berry Model: $\sqrt{\frac{K^* c}{R(\theta)}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{M_w}} \left(1 + \frac{8\pi^2 n_0^2 R_g^2}{3\lambda^2}\sin^2(\theta/2)\right)$

Recommended for very large macromolecules, microgels, or branched polymers ($R_g > 50\text{ nm}$).

Baseline Correction & Solvent Window Taring

Accurate MALS analysis requires subtracting the dark current and solvent background scattering before the protein elutes.

  • Solvent Window (0.5 to 2.5 mL): A clean, pre-void volume region where only pure running buffer flows through the optical cells.
  • Linear Baseline: Draws a straight baseline connecting peak start and end points, compensating for gradual lamp drift or gradient rise.
  • Zero Baseline: Uses absolute Y = 0 as reference level.

How Light Scattering & Molecular Weight Works

1. Why retention time on the Ã„KTA can deceive you

On the Ã„KTA column, small molecules get delayed inside bead pores while big complexes rush through. But floppy or elongated proteins take up extra space, so they elute early even if they are light! Light scattering measures pure physical weight regardless of shape.

2. The Flashlight Analogy (How MALS works)

Shining a laser through your protein is like pointing a flashlight into mist vs. dust motes. Small monomers scatter a little bit of light; large complexes or aggregates flash brightly and scatter massive amounts of light!

3. Why you need Concentration + Light Scattering

Scattered Light = Concentration × Molecular Weight. Dividing the Light Scattering signal by the Refractive Index (dRI) / UV concentration cancels out concentration, yielding the exact Molecular Weight ($M_w$ in kDa).

4. How Stoichiometry Predictor Works (Lego Bricks)

If your MET2TAI subunit is 48 kDa and SUMO tag is 12 kDa (total 60 kDa), an integrated peak with $M_w \approx 120\text{ kDa}$ matches 2 × 60 kDa with 0% error → labeled automatically as Homodimer (2x)!
